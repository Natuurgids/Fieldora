# Fieldora vision

## Purpose

Fieldora helps people turn observations and media from the natural world into durable evidence, knowledge, and research without giving up control of the underlying material.

A person should be able to use Fieldora for a private local library, structured observations, identification and learning, or extend the same evidence into governed projects, dossiers, facilities, knowledge records, and collaborative scientific work.

Scientific participation is an extension of personal use, not a condition for it.

## One platform, multiple adapters

Fieldora is not defined by one user interface. The native desktop application and the web/server application are adapters over the same domain and application semantics. A project, dossier, observation, evidence relationship, permission decision, or provenance rule should not acquire a different meaning merely because it is accessed through Qt or a browser.

Deployment may range from one offline workstation to a Docker-based server or Kubernetes environment. Deployment scale may change; the meaning of the data must remain stable.

## Evidence first

Original media and source material are evidence. Fieldora preserves provenance and stable identity while allowing observations, annotations, classifications, measurements, relationships, dossiers, and derived artifacts to evolve around that evidence.

Organizing evidence must not silently rewrite it. A dossier may reference media without becoming the owner of that media. A project may organize scientific work without redefining Library ownership. Derived data should remain distinguishable from originals.

## Offline-first, connected when useful

Fieldora should remain useful without continuous external connectivity. Local evidence, installed capabilities, and authorized workflows must not depend on a cloud service merely to open or interpret the user's data.

Connectivity can add collaboration, external services, updates, storage nodes, maps, taxonomy, and computational resources. These are extensions of a functioning local model, not substitutes for it.

## Governed collaboration

When Fieldora is used by teams or institutions, collaboration is explicit and governed. Organization isolation, identity, contracts, policy, roles, attributes, object grants, audit, and service trust are part of the platform architecture.

The goal is not to make personal evidence public by default. Sharing and scientific contribution are deliberate actions with traceable authority.

## Modular growth

Fieldora is designed as a modular platform. Library, Science, Project, Dossier, Whiteboard, Observation, Knowledge, Facility, Administration, storage, AI, mapping, and future capabilities keep clear ownership boundaries and communicate through explicit contracts.

New modules should extend the platform without requiring a new definition of existing evidence or bypassing established governance.

## AI assists; people remain accountable

AI may suggest identifications, relationships, enrichments, summaries, or other derived information. Suggestions must remain distinguishable from confirmed evidence and user decisions. AI must not silently overwrite authoritative information or erase provenance.

## Long-term direction

Fieldora should support long-lived evidence and research rather than optimize only for the current release. Compatibility, migration, export, recovery, transparent formats, explicit ownership, and replaceable adapters are therefore product features.

The long-term platform can span personal workstations, field systems, institutional servers, offline environments, and specialist scientific modules while retaining a common evidence and governance model.

> Fieldora begins with evidence and curiosity, grows through observation and research, and enables collaboration without surrendering provenance, ownership, or scientific accountability.
